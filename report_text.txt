Aim

To design a feeder protection scheme for a 3-bus radial power system using Inverse Definite
Minimum Time (IDMT) overcurrent relays, to coordinate the relays so that the downstream relay
operates first and the upstream relay acts as backup, and to verify the design using simulation
data generated in MATLAB.

to Bus 2 and Line 2 connects Bus 2 to Bus 3. Loads are connected at Bus 2 and Bus 3. Relay
R1 protects Line 1 and is located at Bus 1; relay R2 protects Line 2 and is located at Bus 2.

Coordination criterion

Fault sweep along the feeders

The fault position was swept along both lines and the operating times of R1 and R2 were
computed at each point (Fig. 2). The minimum CTI over all faults on Line 2 is 0.354 s (occurring

everywhere on Line 2.

6.4

Time-domain simulation

A three-phase fault was applied at Bus 3 at t = 0.5 s. Each relay is modelled as a disk integrator
that accumulates ∆t/top (I) while the current is above pick-up and trips when the integrator
reaches 1. The circuit breaker opens 0.05 s after the trip command.
 R2 trips 0.439 s after fault inception, which matches the analytical value of 0.439 s.
 The fault is cleared at t = 0.989 s, after which the current through R1 falls to the Bus 2
load current (157.5 A, below pick-up).
 R1 reaches only about 47 % of its operating travel and does not trip, so only Line 2 is
isolated and Bus 2 keeps supply.

Discussion
 Both pick-up currents are above 125 % of normal load, so the relays do not trip on load,
and below the minimum fault current, so they pick up for the weakest fault.
 The most critical coordination point is the high-current fault just beyond R2, where the
two characteristics are closest. Coordinating at the far-end fault only is not sufficient.
 R1 takes 0.68 s to clear a fault at the Bus 2 busbar. This is acceptable for a simple radial
feeder, but the slow clearing near the source can be reduced by adding an instantaneous
(high-set) element on R1 set above the maximum fault current at Bus 2.
 Limitations: the study neglects fault resistance, CT saturation, DC offset and earth faults,
and uses RMS phasor values rather than an electromagnetic transient model.

8

Conclusion

An IDMT (IEC Standard Inverse) overcurrent protection scheme was designed for a 3-bus radial
system. Relay R2 was set to PS = 0.90, TMS = 0.10 and relay R1 to PS = 0.80, TMS = 0.15.

Calculations, a fault sweep along both feeders and a time-domain simulation confirm that R2
clears Line 2 faults first, R1 acts as backup, and the minimum coordination time interval is
0.354 s, which is greater than the required 0.3 s.
